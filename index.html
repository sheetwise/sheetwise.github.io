Units

Units that check your work

Type 75 kg and 9.81 m/s² and the force comes out as 735.75 N. Ask for it in kN or lbf instead. Add 2 m to 30 cm and get 2.3 m. If you add volts to amps, the line turns red and tells you why.

Solving

Solve what a calculator cannot

A diode and a resistor from 5 V: two equations, two unknowns, one of them exponential. Write the equations under Given, call Find, and MathSheet returns 0.555 V and 4.445 mA - with their units.

More about solving →

Control loops

Bode plots with crossover and phase margin

The loop gain of a buck converter with a Type III compensator, plotted straight from the transfer function. The plot reads off the crossover frequency (33.65 kHz) and the phase margin (55.9°) for you. Change a part and watch them move.

More about graphs →

3D plots

Surfaces, contours and vector fields

Type a function of two variables into a 3D plot and drag to turn it. The same worksheet can show contour maps, bar charts, curves in space and vector fields.

Excel

Save a sheet as an Excel workbook: every value becomes a named cell, every result a live formula, every graph a real chart. This chart was drawn by LibreOffice Calc from the workbook MathSheet saved for the buck converter sheet; the crossover and phase margin are live cells beside it.

Why a worksheet

Calculations you can read, check and hand on

A calculator gives you a number but forgets how you got it. A spreadsheet hides the formula inside a cell. In MathSheet the formula is the page: you see the equation, the values and the answer together, with units, in the order you worked it out.

  • Place equations, text and plots anywhere on the page
  • Results update the moment an input changes
  • Mistakes are marked in red with a plain-English explanation
  • Works offline - your files stay on your computer
